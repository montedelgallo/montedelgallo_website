Every Sunday we look at what lit up Hacker News and pick the stories that matter to people building software and running businesses. This week the theme is one: AI promises a lot, but the real work lives where no benchmark reaches. We look at it with pragmatic eyes, the way David Heinemeier Hansson teaches.

## Agents generate more code, not more software

Ars Technica picked up a feeling many of us had. Coding agents write more code, but they do not ship more finished software. The numbers grow, the deliveries stay flat. That is the core point: a fuller repository is not a solved problem. When AI speeds up writing, review becomes the bottleneck. And review cannot be delegated, because it needs judgment. Before you count the lines generated, watch how many reach production without damage. ([source](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/10/ai-coding-agents-generate-more-code-but-not-more-software/))

## 90% of the work escapes the benchmarks

A Mercor survey puts a number on the gap. Almost 90% of US working hours involve tasks that AI benchmarks do not cover well. The tools shine on abstract, clean problems, the ones you can score. Real work is messy, fragmented and full of context. The lesson for companies is not to drop AI. It is to stop choosing it on benchmarks and start measuring it on your own flow. The only test that counts is yours. ([source](https://www.mercor.com/research/labor-index/benchmark-map/))

## Banks are offloading the AI datacenter debt

The surge in AI infrastructure spending has an uncomfortable side. Banks are now trying to lighten the debt they had backed to fund the datacenters. When demand for chips stops growing at the same pace, capital becomes a burden. It is the classic cycle: excitement, investment, then reality. It does not mean AI collapses. It means the race for raw compute is not a business strategy. Building tools that solve problems beats piling up power. ([source](https://startupfortune.com/banks-are-now-trying-to-offload-the-ai-data-center-debt-they-just-underwrote/))

## AI is a tool, and using it well is a skill

The most shared article of the week announces nothing new. It starts from a simple truth: AI is a tool. Like an engine or a language, it is only worth what the person using it wants from it. Those who treat AI as magic dig deep holes. Those who treat it as a utensil fold it into their craft. The difference is not in the tool, it is in the hand that holds it. That is good news for people who work: skill is still the advantage. ([source](https://yyhh.org/blog/2026/10/ai-is-a-tool/))

## The price of intelligence drops under a psychological threshold

On the practical side, the price keeps falling. DeepSeek released a new model and a document-retrieval search at $0.75 per thousand requests. When an expensive operation becomes almost free, the economy of every tool changes. The barriers to entry drop, and even a small company can afford serious AI components. Cost is no longer the obstacle. The judgment with which you apply the models is. ([DeepSeek Flash](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50129045), [DeepSeek search](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50138531))
